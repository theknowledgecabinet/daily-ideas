takeaways:
- Small changes to where things sit and how they are labelled can shift behaviour without changing the options or the person
- Eye level is where the automatic choice is made; rushed people pick the convenient thing, not the best thing, so put the thing you want reached for there
- This works anywhere, from a hospital cafeteria to a kitchen drawer or a desk, because the same shortcut is running when you choose what to eat, buy or do by default
caveats: This was one study at a single hospital cafeteria over six months. The team note that the changes were much more pronounced than at the two control cafeterias, which is reassuring, but they also say future analysis is planned to see whether the effect holds over time. It is also a cafeteria, where a queue and a register shape the choice; your home kitchen and your habits may respond differently.
try_this: Spend ten minutes rearranging one shelf or drawer where you make a repeated choice. Put the thing you want to reach for at eye level and move the thing you want to avoid out of the way or below. Then just watch what happens over a few days.

Anne Thorndike and a team from Massachusetts General Hospital ran a six month study in a busy hospital cafeteria. The aim was to nudge people toward healthier choices without changing the menu or lecturing anyone. Two changes did most of the work.

First, every food and drink got a coloured label: green for the healthiest items, yellow for the middling, red for those with little or no nutritional value. People were told to eat green things often and red things rarely. The registers logged each sale by colour. Red items dropped 9.2 percent, red drinks fell 16.5 percent, and green items rose 4.5 percent, with green drinks up 9.6 percent.

Second, they rearranged the displays. Water, diet drinks and low fat dairy went to eye level; red drinks went below it. Extra bottles of water were placed near the hot food stations, where thirsty people already stood. Compared with two satellite cafeterias where nothing changed, the effect was far stronger where the measures were in place.

The takeaway is about the environment doing the work. The cafeteria was full of rushed people who do not stand around weighing options. When the healthy choice is the easy one, the visible one, the one at eye level, it tends to win without anyone having to summon willpower.
